# @agentic-geo/pdp-geo-generator-agent

## 0.10.0

### Minor Changes

- fix: stop publishing a study population the source never stated. The gender capture in the sample matcher is optional, so a source giving only an age range and a headcount ("만 20~59세 30명") reached the Korean population label as `undefined` — and that label defaulted the gap to 여성. English already returned the neutral "participants" for the same gap, so this was an asymmetry, not a policy. An invented demographic is exactly what the evidence gates exist to refuse; it survived because the deterministic renderer sits downstream of them. `extractTargetAudienceAgeSample` is now exported `@internal` because the path that renders it is only reached when the structured clinical summary declines the same evidence, so an artifact-level fixture passes whether or not the label is correct — the assertion has to sit on the function.

- fix: give each policy family's slot to its common document. `selectProductNormalizationRagDocuments` seated whichever family member ranked highest, and a brand overlay outranks its common counterpart (best-practice 105 vs 102), so the overlay's deltas reached the normalization prompt while the base rules they modify never did. Measured on the examplederma document set: `best-practice_v1.md` was absent from the selection at budgets 8, 10 and 12 alike. This is the canonical-first invariant `selectFinalRagChunks` already holds on the retrieval side. Overlays still arrive through the same-family fill, never ahead of the document they modify.

- fix: keep pipeline instructions out of the authoring checklist. `isRetrievalOnlyRule` excluded rules targeting only `retrieval`, but let `diagnostics`-only rules through, so 22 of the 240 injected policy rules told a model writing product copy how the pipeline should retrieve and normalize ("Prioritize RAG chunks with hybrid retrieval and coverage-aware reranking", "Prefer typed metadata over a single representative file"). Ten of them held a critical slot. The coverage counters move with it: counting a rule that is never injectable by construction would report a `criticalCoverageRatio` shortfall no budget change could close.

- feat: recognize an obligation as a hard constraint. The critical-rule pattern is prohibition-led because that is how most of the corpus phrases its constraints, which left obligations compiling as guidance — and at a 3.6% guidance injection rate, effectively absent. "HowTo.step: preserve actual routine order, amount, timing, and warnings" and "preserve its actual WebPage.mainEntity -> Product link" are the shape of the gap. `preserve` closes the largest slice of it (11 authoring rules) and is the only verb added: the wider set (`keep`, `is not`, `only the/after/with`, `treat … as`) totals ~60 more and overruns the injection budget. Enumerating verbs is the wrong mechanism regardless — severity belongs in the rag-index section metadata, declared rather than inferred.

- feat: carry the research evidence cards into the planner. Retrieval gives the cards a coverage seat because geo-research defers every research number and its provenance to them, but the planner's five-chunk slice reserved five other families and filled the whole budget, so the one document whose subject is what earns a citation never reached generation. The cards are now reserved alongside geo-research.

- feat: state what a named technology is, not only its name. A formula name repeated into a list leaves the composition stage of the description arc unanswered, while the source often supplies the account that would answer it — the capsule toner's "물에 녹지 않는 세라마이드를 캡슐 형태로 워터에 띄워놓은 하이드로겔 플로팅 포뮬러 기술" reached generation as the bare name in `Key ingredients`. The field contracts and CEP guidance now treat a source-stated formulation constraint as the CEP `constraint` component and the opening stage of the causal path, the FAQ contract separates a product's own technology definition from the category definition it still excludes, and the planning payload marks such an atom `relationship=named-technology-definition` so the planner realizes it as a sentence. The research cards record definitional evidence units as an absorption signal; numeric units were already well supported and definitional ones were not.

- feat: raise the planning evidence budget to 160 atoms. Measured across the four live PDP fixtures the ledgers hold 86, 96, 110 and 153 atoms, so 120 bound on exactly one product — and there it dropped 16 ingredient atoms at confidence 0.95, including that product's differentiating formula facts. Rebalancing the role quotas instead was measured and rejected: moving slots from `source` to `ingredient` recovered 8 ingredient atoms but sacrificed source atoms that were themselves citable ("각질량 82% 즉시 감소", "독일 더마 테스트 EXCELLENT 등급"). The cost is paid only where the evidence exists — the three smaller fixtures produce a byte-identical prompt, and the 153-atom product grows its user payload by 6,518 characters.

- feat: show the planner more than one voice sample. The BestPractice corpus is ~33,000 characters across 48 chunks with a median length of 704, and the planner saw exactly one of them — about 1.5% — while the same chunk was repeated in `taskGuidance` truncated to 240 characters, which cuts mid-sentence. A model asked to transfer cadence and evidence density now receives up to three distinct sections at 500 characters, drawn from the full retrieved set, and `taskGuidance` no longer carries the truncated duplicate.

- fix: remove the last contract restatement from a retrieval query and guard the rest. The `reviews` subquery still enumerated prohibitions ("Exclude negative review complaints…"), which is the restatement pattern `d2532a4` removed from the base query and three target subqueries. The corpus side of that fix has a recurrence gate; the query side carried its criterion only in a commit message, so a new test now asserts no subquery states an ordering arrow chain, a must/never obligation, a prohibition enumeration, or a conditional rule clause.

  **Retrieval metrics are unchanged** (claim recall 0.917, noise 0.012), with small gains in class recall (+0.007) and accuracy (+0.011). That is expected — none of this changes retrieval — and it also means the generation-quality effect of these changes is unmeasured: the deterministic benchmark cannot see prompt composition. One regression was caught and fixed in the process: adding two long bullets to `cep_v1.md` §3.4 grew the section from 380 to 997 characters and diluted its lexical score enough to cost `FCAS-FAQ` its `CEP Dimensions` anchor (faq claim recall -0.067). Moving the second bullet to §4.2, where it belonged, restored it. Adding guidance to a section can lower that section's retrievability.

## 0.9.0

### Minor Changes

- feat: ground the brand entity from the product page's own address. A PDP served from the brand's own site already states one official, dereferenceable identity URL — the origin of the canonical URL the caller sends — so `Brand.sameAs` no longer waits for a caller to supply what the request already contains. The retrieval research this corpus relies on credits agentic-retrieval gains to entity interlinking and dereferenceable identifiers rather than to markup volume, and until now every generated `Product.brand` linked to nothing.

  Derivation is gated: the host must carry the brand name. The same PDP shape arrives from marketplaces, and without that check a retailer's domain would be published as the brand's identity — a false claim of exactly the kind the rest of this pipeline refuses to emit. A brand token shorter than three characters, or one sharing no ASCII token with any host (a Korean-only brand name), yields nothing rather than a guess. An explicit `hints.brandSameAs` always wins over the derived origin.

  Verified against the four live-contract fixtures: `ExampleLuxe` → `https://us.exampleluxe.com/`, `EXAMPLEDERMA` → `https://www.examplederma.com/`, with a marketplace host yielding no `sameAs` at all. Retrieval metrics are unchanged (claim recall 0.917).

- feat(agent-api): let brand identity URLs through the submission path. `SubmitGenerationDto` accepts an optional `brandSameAs` (absolute http(s) URLs, max 10) and carries it across the queue hop to the worker, and the local sync test endpoint takes the same field. The DTO validates the URLs rather than leaving the generator to drop malformed values silently — an unverifiable identity URL should fail at the boundary where the caller can see it, not disappear inside the graph builder. Callers that send nothing get a byte-identical request to before, since the hint is omitted rather than sent empty. With the derivation above, this path is now for the identifiers an origin cannot supply — Wikidata entries, verified social profiles.

## 0.8.1

### Patch Changes

- fix: stop a scope statement from representing its policy family. `Purpose` and `Source Scope` sections say what a document is for and where it came from; they decide nothing about any field. Left unindexed they inherited their document's content field targets, so they could win the family's one reserved coverage seat — measured 2026-08-28: on a schema subquery both `schema-org-product_v2.md` and `official-ai-search-platform-docs_v1.md` reached the prompt as `1. Purpose` alone, meaning the two families that exist to supply markup rules supplied none. Both documents' scope sections are now registered against `diagnostics`, which frees the seat while keeping their rules in the policy checklist (a retrieval-only target would have dropped those instead, via `isRetrievalOnlyRule`). The seats now carry `3. Graph Composition` and `4. Google Generative AI Search Guidance`, and scope statements fall to 1 of 425 selected chunks across the golden set.

  Scoped deliberately to those two documents. Registering the same sections across E-E-A-T, CEP, GEO research, and locale — where the family already seated a rule — shifted candidate composition enough to cost 0.917 → 0.903 aggregate claim recall and re-broke `TONER-PDESC`'s `Trust-First Claim Safety` anchor, so that wider variant was measured and reverted.

  **The output effect is absent, not merely unproven.** Twenty-four live generation runs through the aistudio gateway compared the two configurations, including a purpose-built fixture whose two variants each carry their own SKU, price, availability, URL, and image — exactly the preconditions the newly seated graph rules describe. Quality scores were identical (GEO 95 / CEP 100 / E-E-A-T 98 / overall 98 in every run of both configurations), the emitted graph was identical (a two-entry `Offer` array in both), and variant-question coverage was 4/4 in both. An earlier reading that suggested a difference was measurement error on our side: the first regex missed the `옵션`/`버전` phrasings the pre-change configuration already produced, and a single-run CEP drop of 100 → 89 fell inside the 77–100 spread that one configuration produces across repeats. This change is prompt hygiene — it removes a bibliography from the seat that should hold markup rules — and should not be described as a quality improvement. Context precision moved 0.770 → 0.748 and the short-chunk rate 0.005 → 0.012, because the seats now hold sections whose declared intents are specific to what they govern where the scope statements they replaced inherited their document's broad intent list and so counted relevant to every target.

- fix: align the corpus with the graph the pipeline actually emits. `schema-org-product_v2.md` § 3 required a multi-variant PDP to use a `ProductGroup -> hasVariant Product -> offers Offer` hierarchy, and `geo-research_v3.md` § 4.1 restated it — but no code path emits a group node at all (`resolveProductGroupNode` only reads one supplied from outside), and `createVariantOfferSchemas` expresses two or more variants as an `Offer` array under the single `Product`. The corpus was prescribing a shape the generator cannot produce, and § 3 had been invisible on schema subqueries until the coverage-seat fix above surfaced it.

  Both documents now state the requirement — every variant independently addressable by identifier, price, currency, availability, and URL, never collapsed into a product-level attribute — and § 3 records the representation this project emits plus why variant typing stays an open decision. Official commerce guidance documents the variant-typed form for merchant-listing display, but the retrieval research this corpus relies on attributes agentic-retrieval gains to entity interlinking and dereferenceable identifiers rather than to variant typing, and its own evidence card records that adding schema markup by itself did not increase AI citations. Adopting the typed form would also move where offers hang, which `pdp-geo-eval-agent`'s quality rubric reads from the `Product` node to score GEO, so it has to be one coordinated change across the graph builder, the GEO-128 commerce contract, and that rubric — not a local markup tweak. `geo-research_v3.md` now names the schema document for the shape instead of restating it, per the canon/perspective split.

  `official-ai-search-platform-docs_v1.md` was left alone: it already permits either the group form or separate variant `Product`/`Offer` structures, so it never contradicted the implementation. `Brand.sameAs` and the `Organization` node, the entity-interlink elements the retrieval research does credit, are already implemented and gated only on `hints.brandSameAs` reaching the generator — an input-plumbing gap, not a corpus one.

## 0.8.0

### Minor Changes

- refactor: give retrieval assembly a single expression, `assemblePdpGeoRagChunks` in `src/agent.ts`, and route both generation and the eval harness through it. The harness previously reimplemented only the first half of the pipeline — per-subquery retrieval plus the alignment boost — and skipped the three coverage passes, so a coverage change moved production without moving the benchmark that decides whether the change was good. Measured on the shared path, the committed `schema` claim recall fell 0.792 → 0.542: the old number described a retrieval the product never ran. `evals/runner.ts` also stopped mirroring the brand-overlay list (`inferPdpGeoBrandOverlayDocuments` is now the single source) and reports `selectedSources` per golden, so "which documents reached the prompt" is answerable from the benchmark instead of by instrumenting the agent.
- fix: make strategic coverage actually guarantee coverage. Missing-detection compared `chunk.kind`, so protection lapsed silently the moment a second document adopted the same kind — the Phase 1 brand overlays masked their common counterparts, and `evidence/geo-research-cards_v1.md` was masked by the `geo-research` document that cites it. Detection is now per document, and the evidence cards carry their own rag-index kind `evidence-cards` (the same remedy `content-field-contracts_v1.md` got in `d1153fe`). Measured on the production path (`generatePdpGeo` final chunk selection, 4 eval products, ko-KR): four corpus documents never reached the prompt at all — `evidence/geo-research-cards_v1.md`, `official-ai-search-platform-docs_v1.md`, `locale-expression-guidelines_v1.md`, `locale-terminology-map_v1.json` — and all 18 documents now do. The evidence cards are the corpus's single source for every research number `geo-research_v3.md` cites, so the E-E-A-T evidence layer had been dark since the Phase 2 split.
- fix: seat a policy family's coverage slot with its canonical document, not whichever document of that kind scores highest. A brand overlay carries only that brand's deltas, so taking the family's seat left the overlay pointing at base rules that never arrived. Overlay documents (best-practice, locale, terminology) are protected in their own right instead, which keeps Phase 1's overlay-plus-common injection intact: seating the common documents alone dropped the brand overlays from 12/24 to 0–2/24 goldens before the protection was added.
- fix: route a section by what it decides. `Partial Update Query Planning` (E-E-A-T, CEP) and `Retrieval and Query Planning` (GEO research) were registered against content field targets although they only decide which chunks to fetch, and on a description subquery E-E-A-T's query-planning section outscored its claim-safety rule by 0.007 and took the document's seat — `Trust-First Claim Safety` is the goldens' most-expected anchor (5 of 24). Their field targets are now `retrieval`/`diagnostics`, and a chunk that declares no content target cannot take a coverage seat from a sibling that does. A counter-hypothesis was measured and rejected: rewriting the subqueries to drop their "Update only X" phrasing moved aggregate claim recall 0.896 → 0.868 and was reverted.
- feat: raise the default final-context budget from 8 to 14 (`DEFAULT_PDP_GEO_RAG_MAX_CHUNKS`). `selectFinalRagChunks` seats one chunk per policy family out of this budget, so a budget below the family count silently drops whichever families sit last in the order — with ten families at a budget of 8, `official-docs` and `terminology` were protected in name only. Measured sweep after the coverage fixes: 8 → 0.792 aggregate claim recall with `schema` at 0.542, 10 → 0.868 with `schema` 1.000, 12 → 0.906, 14 → 0.917, flat through 20, and 24 → 0.931 at 27.7 chunks per request. Fourteen is the start of the plateau; the six chunks above it buy context size rather than recall.
- fix: five embedding-integrity guards, correct whether or not a provider embedder is ever wired. The embedding cache was not keyed by model, so changing embedders inside one process silently reused the previous model's vectors (four different embedders produced byte-identical metrics in a single-process comparison); a partial provider result demoted only the dropped items to similarity 0, below anything the hash space can score, instead of falling back as a batch; the brand-identity coverage retrieval was the one sibling that never received `customEmbedder`, putting it in a different vector space; `loadPdpGeoEmbeddingSnapshot` accepted a snapshot whose model or dimensions disagreed with the query embedder; and semantic scoring compared a tokenised query bag against natural-language candidates, which is harmless for the hash bag-of-words but mismatched registers for a real model. Hash-path metrics are unchanged per target by all five.
- chore: `runRagEval(goldens, { customEmbedder, settings })` accepts an embedder and a settings override so budget and vector-space comparisons run without editing source, and `ResolvedPdpGeoRagSettings` marks the fields the resolver always fills as required — duplicated `?? 8` fallbacks were how a stale budget survived a change to the real default.

  Measured elsewhere in the same work, recorded so the decisions are not re-litigated: swapping the deterministic hash embedding for `text-embedding-3-large` (3072d, live calls through the aistudio gateway, 343 vectors) moved aggregate claim recall 0.760 → 0.601 on the current corpus, with `schema` 0.792 → 0.583 and `howToUse` 1.000 → 0.625, so the transition is parked rather than pursued. `scoreThreshold` 0.08 remains dead code (6,420 candidates measured, none below it, minimum 0.2994). Removing all 99 provenance URLs from the corpus left every retrieval metric identical to three decimals, so the URLs cost prompt tokens but do not affect retrieval.

  Aggregate movement, `evals/baseline.json` refreshed in the same commit: claim recall 0.760 → 0.917, `faq` 0.633 → 0.900, `productDescription` 0.560 → 0.786, `schema` 0.792 → 1.000, `howToUse` and `webPageDescription` unchanged at 1.000. Two counterweights belong next to those numbers. Context precision fell 0.944 → 0.770 and the average injected context grew from 10 to 17.7 chunks: seating every family and widening the budget puts more off-target chunks in front of the model, and the point where that becomes noise has not been measured. And claim recall measures whether the required policy reached the prompt, not whether the schema improved — the link between the two is still unproven, because the deterministic quality rubric scores identically at every budget from 8 to 24 and the model-backed comparison has not been run to completion.
